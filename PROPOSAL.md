# ShadowPass — Product Proposal

## Problem Statement
In traditional blockchain ecosystems (such as Ethereum and EVM-compatible networks), implementing allowlist gating for token sales, private DAO governance, or exclusive community access requires storing raw public addresses on a public smart contract or verifying signatures on-chain. This creates a severe privacy leak: every observer can trace which specific address claimed access, track their wallet balance and transaction history, and permanently map off-chain human identities to on-chain pseudonyms. EVM-style allowlists force users to choose between exclusive access and basic personal financial privacy.

## Proposed Solution
**ShadowPass** is a zero-knowledge private allowlist dApp built on the Midnight blockchain that replaces public address lists with cryptographic identity commitments stored in private state. Admins register blinded identity commitments into an off-chain Merkle tree, publishing only the 32-byte Merkle root (`allowlistRoot`) to the public ledger. Users generate a Zero-Knowledge proof locally via Midnight's Compact language, proving that they possess a secret key and blinding salt corresponding to an entry in the allowlist Merkle tree. Upon verification, the smart contract unlocks an `accessGranted` public boolean status without ever learning or exposing the prover's identity, wallet address, or position in the allowlist.

## Why Midnight's Privacy Model is Essential
Midnight's native privacy architecture—combining Compact smart contracts, client-side witness evaluation, and public/private state separation—is essential to making ShadowPass possible. On an EVM chain, either the allowlist addresses are completely visible in contract storage or expensive, custom ZK circuits (e.g. Tornado-style nullifier schemes) must be manually engineered with complex off-chain prover infrastructure. Midnight natively bifurcates state: secret keys and Merkle sibling paths remain strictly private in the user's local witness context, while the Compact contract enforces state transitions on the public ledger (`accessGranted = true`). An observer of the public ledger can verify that a legitimate, authorized member unlocked access, but can *never* deduce which member proved access, their wallet address, or their private balances.
